Pain scale (0–10)

Pick the number that best matches how pain affects you, and use the same scale every time.

0No painNothing at all.
1Very mildBarely noticeable; you forget about it most of the time.
2MildNoticeable but doesn't get in the way of anything.
3UncomfortableDistracting at times, but you can do everything you planned.
4ModerateHard to ignore for long; you can still work and do daily tasks.
5DistractingAlways on your mind; you can do most things, with effort.
6DistressingMakes it hard to concentrate; you start cutting back on activities.
7SevereStops you doing normal activities; hard to sleep.
8Very severeHard to talk or think about anything else.
9ExcruciatingYou can barely move or speak because of the pain.
10Worst imaginableThe worst pain you can imagine.

Why the number alone isn't enough

Two people can mean very different things by "6 out of 10". That's why each number above is tied to what pain stops you doing. When you give a score, add one example – "7, I had to leave work early" – and your doctor will understand far more. Our guide on how to describe pain to your doctor covers the other things to mention.

Use the same scale every time

A pain score is most useful as a trend. Rate at roughly the same times each day, use these same descriptions, and record your best, usual and worst scores. FAQ

What is a 0–10 pain scale?

It's a simple way of rating pain where 0 is no pain and 10 is the worst pain you can imagine. It's widely used by GPs, specialists, physios and hospitals.

What does 5 out of 10 pain mean?

On this scale, 5 is pain that's always on your mind but still lets you do most things with effort. Add an example of what it affects so your doctor understands what 5 means for you.

Is 7 out of 10 pain severe?

Yes. Scores of 7 and above usually describe severe pain that stops normal activities. If you have sudden severe pain, chest pain or trouble breathing, call 000.

Can I print this pain scale chart?
